The two formal filings published on this site — the complaint against X Corp. and the submission on the minors' discrimination guidelines gap — both rest on a longer legal foundation than either document sets out in full. Behind them is a complete legal memorandum: a detailed, article-by-article analysis of the Digital Services Act's enforcement architecture as it applies to minors' rights and non-discrimination.

## What it covers

The memorandum works through DSA Articles 22, 28, 34, 35, and 37 in sequence — trusted-flagger status, the Article 28 minors' protection guidelines, systemic risk assessment, mitigation obligations, and independent auditing — mapping how each provision is meant to interact with the others, and where the current implementing guidance leaves that interaction undefined. It cross-references the relevant provisions of the EU Charter of Fundamental Rights, works through the enforcement precedent set by the Commission's first DSA fines, and closes with a practical implementation roadmap: specific, sequenced steps a regulator, platform compliance team, or civil society organisation could take to close the gap this research identifies.

It is written for people who need to act on this analysis, not just read about it — regulators drafting or revising guidance, platform legal and trust & safety teams assessing their own exposure, and civil society or academic institutions building enforcement strategy or litigation around the same gap.

## Why it isn't published here

Everything else on this site — the articles, the formal filings themselves — is free to read, cite, and use. The memorandum is different: it's a substantial, standalone piece of legal work, and it's available on a licensing basis rather than as a free download. That keeps the underlying research sustainable without changing anything about what's published openly elsewhere on this site.
